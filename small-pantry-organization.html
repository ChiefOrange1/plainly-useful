Small Pantry Organization: A 5-Step Method (Plus Containers Worth Considering)

Small pantries fail for two reasons: you can't see what you have, and things don't have assigned spots. The method below fixes both before you buy anything.

Step 1: Empty and sort

Take everything out. Toss expired items. Group the rest: baking, grains and pasta, snacks, canned goods, oils and sauces, spices.

Step 2: Zone by frequency

Eye level holds daily items. Up high holds backups and holiday-only items. The floor or bottom shelf holds heavy things.

Step 3: Decant only what you use often

Clear, stackable containers help most with staples you go through quickly (flour, rice, oats, cereal). Measure shelf height and depth first and keep a few inches of clearance for lids.

Step 5: Label and keep a 'use first' bin

Label shelves rather than only containers, so items go back to the right place. A small 'use first' bin keeps open packages from getting lost.

FAQ

Should I decant everything into matching containers?
No. Decant the staples you refill often; leave rarely-used items in their packaging to save time and money.
How do I measure for pantry containers?
Measure shelf depth, width and the clear height between shelves, then leave room to lift lids.
